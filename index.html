A Letter From the Founder

Why I’d Rather Lose Your January Order Than Lie to You in July

At 5:30 last Monday morning I was standing in New Covent Garden market, smelling strawberries.

Not looking at them. Smelling them. A strawberry picked at peak ripeness smells like jam from three feet away. A strawberry picked early, so it can survive a warehouse, smells of nothing. That smell is the entire difference between what I make and most of what this city calls gelato.

My name is Marco Castellano. I trained as a gelatiere in Italy, where I was taught that gelato begins with an ingredient, not a catalogue. When I moved to London I found beautiful marble counters selling “artisan strawberry” in the middle of winter. Nobody could tell me where the strawberries came from. Eventually I understood why: there were no strawberries.

So I built Gelatorchard on one rule that costs me money every single month: if a fruit isn’t in season in Britain, we don’t make that flavour. No powder, no purée from a tin, no “natural flavouring”. Not even in February, when it would be easy, and cheap, and nobody would ever know.

Here’s what that rule does to my menu: in January I can offer you exactly one fresh fruit flavour. One. Yorkshire forced rhubarb, grown in the dark, picked by candlelight. A sensible businessman would quietly reach for the powder. I’d rather show you a short menu than a long lie, because that short January menu is the only proof I have that the July strawberry in your freezer is exactly what I say it is.

Marco

P.S. Every bag we sell carries a QR code to the exact batch it came from: the farmer’s name, the field, the hour it was picked, the production log. I didn’t add it to impress you. I added it so I could never cheat, even if I wanted to.
