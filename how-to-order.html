The Process

One Week. Five Steps. No Warehouse.

Every date below is the actual rhythm of the lab, not a marketing promise.

1 Monday 10am – Tuesday 6pm

Your window

This is your window. Order here. That’s it. After 6pm on Tuesday? Your order waits for next week’s drop. Nothing jumps the rhythm. Every drop is capped at 42 servings: when they’re gone, they’re gone.

Order in the window
2 Tuesday evening

I text the farm

I count your orders and text Tom: “I need 5kg of strawberries and 3kg of raspberries, delivered Thursday evening.” Tom says yes or no. Nobody in between.

3 Thursday evening

Fruit arrives

I check the temperature, look for bruises, and write it all down. Yes, really: it becomes the batch log you can read. Straight into the fridge.

4 Friday morning

I make your batches

Exact quantity, nothing left over. My bench machine churns 750g at a time, about an hour per cycle: a full drop is a six-hour morning. Bags and cups are filled at extraction, then straight into the freezer at −18°C.

5 Saturday or Sunday

You eat it

You pick a slot. I deliver to Canary Wharf, Poplar and South Quay. Made yesterday, not last month.

Fair Questions

“But Why Can’t You Just…?”

…make stock in advance?

Gelato oxidises. After four weeks at −18°C the texture breaks down. If I made twenty batches on Monday and you ordered on Saturday, you’d get old product. Not acceptable.

…order from a wholesale supplier?

Wholesale means flavour powder. We don’t use it. Fresh fruit means Tom in Kent picked it this week, not a container from Spain three weeks ago.

…get it to me urgently?

You don’t need gelato urgently. You want something cold and sweet that’s worth the wait. So you order Monday and eat Saturday. Life moves slower when you’re not chasing next-day delivery.
